Reading the room

Three buyer types will arrive at Deeply's first marketing page. The surface has to do all three jobs.

A bilateral relationship product attracts buyers in different mental states. The site has to convert each of them differently. The blocks below are designed around these three.

Strategic
Buyer 01 · The hurting partner
"Something's wrong, we need help"
Found Deeply at 11pm after a fight. Wants the conversation to stop hurting and start moving. Decision: try it tonight or wait until next month. The site has to land "this is for tonight" in 5 seconds.
Highest intent 85%
Buyer 02 · The proactive partner
"Therapy waitlist is 6 weeks"
Already in counseling-curious mode. Read about Deeply somewhere. Wants to understand what makes it different from BetterHelp, Lasting, Paired. Will read carefully before committing.
Comparison mode 68%
Buyer 03 · The skeptical partner
"Why should I trust this with my marriage?"
Their partner just sent them a magic link. They're cautious. Wants to know: is the AI playing therapist? Is this safe? Who's behind it? Trust signals decide.
Trust gate 52%
Three things you already have to work with
A real wedge. Bilateral signed commitments are not what BetterHelp, Lasting, Paired, or Arya do. Most relationship apps are solo-input or insight-only. Deeply is structured-conversation-to-commitment. A validation number that means something. 4.56/5 for "signed resolution scored highest of any metric tested" is a real PMF signal pre-launch. A founder who ships. Set Labs / Index Cooperative was real. Telly was real. The self-taught engineer-founder origin story (freeCodeCamp essay, 5K+ reactions) is a brand asset. None of these need building. They need surfacing on the first page.

A REAL EXAMPLE BEATS ABSTRACT EXPLANATION · AI IS TRANSPARENT, NOT MAGICAL
DESIGN NOTES
The example matters. Pick something universal (dinner, screen time, chores, money) so the viewer instantly sees themselves. Show the AI's translation literally so the viewer understands what Deeply does that they couldn't do alone. The signed-and-dated outcome is the moment the viewer thinks "oh, this is different from just venting."
03
Trust signals for a mental-health-adjacent product
Therapist endorsements, validation methodology, privacy commitments, what-we-are-NOT statement.
RISKY · OVERCLAIM
"AI couples therapy that works."

Reads as "AI playing therapist." Triggers immediate skepticism from Buyer 03 and regulatory concern.
HONEST · EARNS TRUST
What we are: a structured conversation tool that turns hard talks into signed commitments.

What we are not: therapy, AI playing therapist, a substitute for crisis support.

If you're in crisis, here's where to go: 988 (US), Samaritans (UK), more.
Three trust badges that ship in week 1
Validation methodology
"Our signed resolution scored 4.56/5 in a 50-couple validation panel. Details: [link]." Show the work.
Therapist endorsements
1-2 licensed couples therapists who reviewed the flow. Their actual quote, their actual name and credential. Earns 80% of the trust the site needs.
Privacy commitment
"Your conversations are encrypted at rest, never used to train models, deleted on request." Plain language, footer-anchored.
04
Bilateral CTA mechanic
"Send invite to your partner" as primary action. Solves the partner-2 problem at the marketing layer.
DEFAULT · SOLO SIGNUP
Primary CTA: "Sign up free"

User signs up alone. Then has to figure out how to invite partner. Most don't. Partner-2 activation rate craters.

Every relationship app fails here.
DEEPLY · BILATERAL CTA
Primary CTA: "Send invite to your partner"

User enters: their email + partner's contact + the conflict in one sentence ("dinner phone use"). Magic link sent. Partner-2 lands at the specific conversation context, not a generic signup page.

Bilateral by default. Solves the structural problem upstream.

Where WSO's product surface
has room to grow.

This is not a conversion audit. WSO is profitable and the funnel works. It's a product-opportunity audit, the kind of thing I'd bring to a first roadmap meeting. The throughline: WSO is sitting on a 17-year data moat and running it across two disconnected stacks. The PM hired into this role is the person who connects the seam and turns the archive into product. Ten findings, grouped four ways.

Audited live · 2026-05-11 · wallstreetoasis.com · founded 2006 · Drupal · 17+ year archive
Reading the room

WSO has two products and three kinds of user.

The free community member, the Academy applicant, and the Academy student are three different relationships with WSO. They live across two stacks that do not share a spine. The PM's job starts here: making the seam between them a designed product, not an accident of history.

Strategic
User 01 · Free community member
Reading the forum, free
Found WSO via a Google search for an IB recruiting question. Reads threads, lurks. The 17-year archive is what they came for. They are the top of the funnel and the least instrumented.
Volume highest
User 02 · Academy applicant
On the waitlist, deciding
Joined the Academy waitlist, got the two free resources, is weighing a $6K-$9K decision. The conversion from User 01 to here is the single most valuable transition WSO has, and it is barely measured.
Leverage the money step
User 03 · Academy student
Paid, in the 12-week program
In the Academy dashboard, working through mentorship and mock interviews. Lives almost entirely on the newer stack. The "Top Job Offer Guaranteed" promise rides on this user's outcome.
Outcome stake the guarantee
Three things already working
The data moat is real and deep. 17 years of forum Q&A, 100,000+ interview and salary datapoints, 10,000+ mock-interview scores. No competitor can buy or build that corpus. The Academy guarantee is a genuine trust signal. "Top Job Offer Guaranteed or your tuition is free," with an under-4% acceptance rate and 88% placement, is a real and defensible promise. The "organic community" brand position is the un-salesy option in a salesy category. It took 17 years to earn and cannot be shortcut. None of these need fixing. The findings below are about turning them into more product.

A
The two-stack seam
Three findings. WSO is two products that don't share a spine.
01

WSO is two products on two stacks. The community site and the Academy don't feel like one company.

Strategic
EVIDENCE
The main site at wallstreetoasis.com and the Academy at /academy run on visibly different systems. Different nav, different visual language, different feel. One is the 19-year Drupal community monolith. The other is a newer Academy platform. A user crossing from one to the other notices the seam.
wallstreetoasis.com/academy A DIFFERENT VISUAL SYSTEM
ACADEMY: DIFFERENT NAV, DIFFERENT SYSTEM FROM THE MAIN SITE
WHY IT MATTERS
The PM hired into this role is the connective tissue across these two stacks. The seam isn't just cosmetic. It's where the most valuable user signal gets lost (Findings 02 and 08). Week one, I'd want an honest map of what lives on which stack and where the handoffs are.
Strategic priority for the PM role the core of the job
02

A community member and an Academy student are probably two separate identity records.

High
EVIDENCE
Two stacks, two systems, near-certainly two identity tables. The community runs on Drupal. The Academy CRM is Keap. A forum power-user and an Academy lead are the same human, but the systems likely can't see that without manual stitching.
WHY IT MATTERS
It means WSO cannot automatically see "this person read 30 IB-recruiting threads this month" as a high-intent Academy lead. The warmest signal in the building is invisible to the team that would act on it. A shared identity spine across Drupal and Keap is the unlock.
03

The Drupal monolith's age is a roadmap constraint nobody has scoped on paper.

Strategic
EVIDENCE
wallstreetoasis.com has run on Drupal since 2006. A 19-year-old codebase. Every new community-side feature either works with the Drupal version or gets built as a bolt-on. That's not a crisis, Drupal is sturdy, but it's a constraint that shapes every roadmap decision.
WHY IT MATTERS
A PM who can talk fluently with senior engineers (the JD's exact phrase) should own an honest assessment: what can Drupal still do well, what should move off it, what's the cost of each path. That's a week-one or week-two deliverable, and it makes every later roadmap call cheaper.
B
The archive is a moat used like a band-aid
Three findings. 17 years of Q&A, underbuilt as product.
04

The AI bot posts in forum threads. That's a band-aid. The real product is "ask the archive."

High · biggest unbuilt product
EVIDENCE
WSO has an AI bot trained on 17+ years of WSO content. Right now it shows up as a participant inside forum threads. Useful, but it's a feature bolted onto the old surface.
THE OPPORTUNITY
The actual product is a discovery layer: "ask the WSO archive." A user types a finance-recruiting question and gets a synthesized answer with cited source threads, instantly. It's the single highest-value unbuilt product WSO has, because the corpus already exists and no competitor can match it. This is Workflow 03 in the next section.
Product opportunity size the moat, made queryable
05

"100,000+ interview & salary datapoints" is a homepage stat. It should be a product surface.

High
EVIDENCE
The homepage says, verbatim, "100,000+ interview & salary datapoints across thousands of firms." It's framed as a credibility number, sitting next to "1,217,427 students enrolled."
wallstreetoasis.com THE MOAT, USED AS A STAT
100,000+ DATAPOINTS: A CREDIBILITY LINE, NOT YET A QUERYABLE PRODUCT
THE OPPORTUNITY
100,000 structured datapoints want to be a sliceable product: a compensation explorer, a firm-by-firm interview-difficulty index, a "what does an analyst at this fund actually make" tool. That's a reason to return monthly, and a natural funnel into Academy. Right now it's a number you read once.
06

Forum search is pre-AI-era in a category where users now expect synthesized answers.

High
EVIDENCE
A 17-year Drupal forum almost certainly does keyword search over threads. In 2026 a user with a recruiting question expects to ask it and get a synthesized answer, the way they would from any AI tool.
THE OPPORTUNITY
Competitors will ship "ask AI" over their content. They have smaller corpora. WSO has the biggest one in the category and should win this decisively, but only if the PM builds the discovery layer (Finding 04) before someone else's good-enough version sets the user expectation.
C
The community-to-Academy funnel
Two findings. The money step exists, but it isn't instrumented.
07

The funnel exists as steps, not as an instrumented product with conversion rates per stage.

High
EVIDENCE
The path is: free community member, then Academy waitlist (with two free resources), then application, then admissions call. Each step is real and intentional.
THE FUNNEL AS IT RUNS TODAY
FREE MEMBER → WAITLIST → APPLICATION → ADMISSIONS CALL → ENROLLED
Four transitions. If each one isn't a measured number with an experiment slot, WSO is flying the most valuable part of the business on instinct.
THE OPPORTUNITY
Instrument every transition. Conversion rate per step, dropoff reasons, an A/B slot at each one. The waitlist-to-application step alone, optimized, moves real revenue given a $6K-$9K price point.
08

The highest-intent Academy leads are invisible, because of the identity seam in Finding 02.

High
EVIDENCE
A free community member reading 30 IB-recruiting threads is the warmest possible Academy lead. But community-side behavior lives on Drupal and the Academy CRM is Keap. The intent signal and the team that would act on it are on different stacks.
THE OPPORTUNITY
Once the identity spine exists (Finding 02), forum behavior becomes a lead score. "This member just read the PE-recruiting megathread three times" becomes a trigger: surface the Academy waitlist to exactly that person, at exactly that moment. The funnel stops being passive.
D
Ops latency
Two findings. The JD asks for ops automation by name. Here's where it goes first.
09

Service latency is already visible to the public. A reviewer reports resume review taking "more than one month."

High · maps to the JD
EVIDENCE
A Trustpilot reviewer reports a resume review took "more than one month" with "slow communication." The JD explicitly asks the PM to "scope and ship internal automations." This is where that work starts, and it's measurable from day one.
THE OPPORTUNITY
Resume-review SLA automation: an intake that timestamps and starts a clock, an AI pre-pass that flags the obvious fixes so the human reviewer starts from an annotated draft, and an SLA dashboard that catches anything aging past target. Month becomes days. Workflow 04 builds this.
Turnaround improvement target month → days
10

Mentor-matching and admissions triage are human-heavy. Both are on-brand automation targets.

Medium-High
EVIDENCE
WSO works with 3,500+ mentors. Matching students to the right mentor, and triaging Academy applications against an under-4% acceptance bar, is human-heavy judgment work that scales linearly with headcount.
THE OPPORTUNITY
An AI-assisted layer: mentor-matching that proposes the top three fits with reasoning a human confirms, and an admissions pre-pass that ranks applications so the human reviewer spends time on the genuine maybes. Not replacing the judgment, removing the sorting. That's the on-brand automation the JD wants.

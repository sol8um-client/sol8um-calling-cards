The AI-promise gap
Three findings. Your JD says AI is core. The homepage says SEO Made Easy.
01

Hero says "SEO for WordPress Made Easy." JD says AI is core to product strategy. The two narratives don't meet on the same page.

Critical · 1 hr
EVIDENCE
Hero verbatim: "SEO for WordPress Made Easy" + "Rank Math is the most powerful way to get BEST SEO tools for WordPress added to your website." Above-the-fold AI mentions: zero. Above-the-fold install badge: "4 Million+." For the AI-PM hire reporting to the CTO, the hero is the highest-leverage lever in week one.
rankmath.com HERO · ABOVE FOLD
HERO PROMISES SEO · NEVER NAMES AI
AI LIVES HERE · A NAV CLICK AWAY

An AI-search-aware founder lands here, scrolls the hero looking for "AI search," "GEO," or "llms.txt." Finds none. Closes the tab. Two minutes later they're reading Yoast's AI page (which has weaker product but louder marketing) and comparing pricing tabs side-by-side. QUICK WIN
Rewrite the hero to do both jobs. "SEO for WordPress, now with AI built in" or "The first SEO plugin built for AI search". Pair it with a sub-headline that lists 2-3 concrete AI features (llms.txt, AI search traffic tracking, RankBot). Keep the install badge. Move the "Made Easy" framing to the third line.

Pricing friction
One finding. Renewal-bump pricing reads dark-pattern-adjacent.
07

PRO $7.99 → $8.99 on renewal. Business +12%. Agency +18%. Either kill the bumps or surface them with rationale.

Medium · 30 min
EVIDENCE
Pricing tiers all show year-1 price prominently with the year-2 renewal price in smaller text. PRO: $7.99 → $8.99 (12.5% bump). Business: $24.99 → $27.99 (12% bump). Agency: $54.99 → $64.99 (18% bump). On a clean otherwise-transparent pricing page, this reads dark-pattern-adjacent.
rankmath.com/pricing RENEWAL BUMPS · ON THE LIVE PAGE
$54.99 → $64.99 ON RENEWAL (+18%)
$24.99 → $27.99 (+12%)
$7.99 → $8.99 (+12.5%)
QUICK WIN
Two paths: (a) Kill the bumps and price flat. Reduces year-1 conversion friction, increases lifetime trust. (b) Surface them transparently with one line: "Most users renew at $X. We're upfront about it." The current half-on state (visible but downplayed) is the worst of both.
